# Draft — `feast_begin` (Chapter I: The Feast of Polydectes)
**Target:** ~250 words | **Sources:** Apollodorus, *Library* 2.4.2–3; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 4.604–5.249
**Status:** Structural draft — rewrite in your own voice before use

---

## Draft

You are on Seriphos. The island is small. Polydectes is its king.

Polydectes wants your mother, Danaë. You are the reason he has not taken her. You are not a soldier or a landowner. You are young and you have nothing. What you have is presence — the fact that you are there, between them. That is all.

Tonight Polydectes holds a feast. The hall is full. The king announces that he is courting Hippodameia and that each man present will contribute a horse as a wedding gift. The guests agree. They have horses. You do not.

Polydectes looks at you. He waits.

You have no horse. You have no land, no wealth, no gift to offer. The room is watching. Polydectes already knows what you will say. He has arranged this moment.

You tell him you will bring him whatever he asks. You say it before you have thought about it.

He asks for the head of Medusa.

The room goes quiet. Polydectes accepts your promise in front of witnesses. There is no taking it back.

You understand, at some point in the silence that follows, that this is what he wanted. A reason to send you away. A reason that sounds like honor.

## Watch for — AI tics to rewrite away from

- "What you have is presence" — abstract in a way that gestures at profundity; flatten it or cut it
- "The room goes quiet" — atmospheric filler; replace with something specific or cut
- "at some point in the silence that follows" — hedged and atmospheric; nail it down
- "sounds like honor" — this is doing interpretive work that belongs in the Author's Note, not the narrative; remove or make literal
- Sentence rhythm in the opening three paragraphs is too even — yours will be different; that's fine
